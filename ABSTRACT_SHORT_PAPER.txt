Synthetic medical data generation requires human-auditable control: latent factors should separate data-specific structure so clinicians can inspect, trust, and responsibly reuse synthetic samples. Building on our prior evolutionary generator work \cite{}, we present an Evolutionary HyperNet-InfoGAN in which PGPE optimizes a compact HyperNetwork that emits the weights of a larger image generator, while a discriminator/Q-network is trained with backpropagation. This creates a non-stationary adversarial setting in which evolutionary search must continually adapt, but in a far smaller and more structured parameter space than direct evolution of the full generator.

We evaluate a shared baseline on MNIST (28x28 grayscale, 10 classes) and on grayscale-converted BloodMNIST (28x28, 8 classes) using a shared-z protocol in which groups of samples share continuous noise while discrete codes vary. This setup enables direct measurement of feature-space code separation ($r_sense$) and within-code variation ($r_intra$). Relative to our earlier PGPE baseline that searched directly over a generator with more than 500k parameters, the HyperNetwork formulation yields better mode coverage, cleaner code allocation, and substantially more stable late-stage training.

On MNIST, the baseline approaches full digit coverage with fewer duplicate-code failures than prior direct search. On BloodMNIST, preliminary baseline results show partial disentanglement and class-consistent morphology, suggesting that HyperNetworks provide a practical intermediate step toward auditable, controllable synthetic medical image generation. These baselines motivate the next stage of this work: introducing cultural-algorithm guidance as a meta-level controller over the PGPE fitness landscape for adaptive fitness weighting and secondary search guidance.
